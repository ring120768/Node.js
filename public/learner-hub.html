🚨 First Steps After a Collision

⚠️ PRIORITY: If anyone is injured, call 999 immediately. Your safety and the safety of others comes first.

1. Stop and Stay Safe

You must stop if you've been involved in an accident, even if it's minor. Failing to stop is a criminal offence in the UK and can result in prosecution.

💡 Safety Tip: If it's safe to do so, move your vehicle to the side of the road. Turn on your hazard lights to warn other drivers.

2. Check for Injuries

  • Check yourself and any passengers for injuries
  • Check the other driver and their passengers
  • Check any pedestrians involved
  • Call 999 if anyone is hurt or the road is blocked

3. Exchange Information

You are legally required to exchange the following information with the other driver:

  • Name and contact details
  • Insurance details (company name and policy number)
  • Vehicle registration number
  • Address
💡 Pro Tip: Use your phone to take a photo of the other driver's insurance certificate and driving licence. This ensures you have accurate information.

4. Document Everything

Take photos and videos of:

  • All vehicle damage from multiple angles
  • The position of vehicles after the collision
  • Road conditions, weather, and lighting
  • Any road signs, traffic lights, or markings
  • Skid marks or debris
  • Any witnesses present

5. Report the Incident

  • Report to your insurance company within 24 hours (even if you weren't at fault)
  • Report to the police within 24 hours if injury or damage occurred
  • Keep a written record of what happened while it's fresh in your memory
⚠️ Important: Never admit fault at the scene, even if you think you were responsible. Let the insurance companies and authorities determine fault based on evidence.

6. Get Witness Details

If there were any witnesses to the accident, get their:

  • Name and contact details
  • Written statement if possible
  • Note what they saw

What NOT to Do

  • ❌ Don't leave the scene without exchanging details
  • ❌ Don't admit fault or apologise
  • ❌ Don't accept cash settlements on the spot
  • ❌ Don't sign any documents unless required by police
  • ❌ Don't post about the accident on social media

🛡️ Defensive Driving Techniques

Defensive driving is about anticipating potential hazards and taking action to avoid accidents before they happen. It's one of the most important skills you'll develop as a driver.

The 2-Second Rule

Always maintain at least a 2-second gap between you and the vehicle in front:

  • Watch the vehicle ahead pass a fixed point (lamp post, bridge, sign)
  • Count "Only a fool breaks the 2-second rule"
  • You should pass the same point after finishing the phrase
  • Double this in wet conditions (4 seconds)
  • Increase to 10 seconds in icy conditions
💡 Remember: The faster you're travelling, the more distance you need to stop safely.

Scanning Ahead

Look 12-15 seconds ahead on the road to identify potential hazards early:

  • Check for brake lights ahead (not just the car in front)
  • Watch for pedestrians near crossings
  • Look for cyclists and motorcyclists
  • Identify merging traffic and junction activity
  • Check road signs for upcoming hazards

Mirror Checks

Check your mirrors every 5-8 seconds:

  • Interior mirror – what's directly behind
  • Side mirrors – check blind spots
  • Before any manoeuvre (MSM: Mirror, Signal, Manoeuvre)
  • Before changing speed or direction

Anticipating Other Drivers

  • Assume other drivers might make mistakes
  • Watch for indicators and brake lights
  • Be prepared for cars to pull out of junctions
  • Give extra space to learners, elderly drivers, and delivery vehicles
  • Watch for reversing lights in car parks

Speed Management

Driving at an appropriate speed isn't just about speed limits:

  • Slow down in residential areas, even if the limit is 30mph
  • Reduce speed in poor weather conditions
  • Adjust speed for road surface conditions
  • Slow down when you can't see far ahead (bends, hills)
⚠️ Remember: Speed limits are maximums, not targets. Drive at a speed that's safe for the conditions.

Handling Aggressive Drivers

  • Don't react to aggressive behaviour
  • Let tailgaters pass when safe to do so
  • Don't make eye contact or gestures
  • Pull over if you feel threatened
  • Report dangerous driving to police on 101 (999 if immediate danger)

Night Driving Safety

  • Use dipped headlights in built-up areas
  • Use full beam on unlit roads (when no traffic ahead)
  • Dip your lights when approaching other vehicles
  • Keep windscreen and lights clean
  • Reduce speed – you can only drive as fast as you can see
💡 Pro Tip: If you're dazzled by oncoming headlights, look towards the left edge of your lane rather than directly at the lights.

🔄 Roundabout Navigation Guide

Roundabouts can be intimidating for learner drivers, but following these simple rules will help you navigate them safely and confidently.

Basic Rules

  • Give way to traffic from the right (already on the roundabout)
  • Traffic flows clockwise
  • Signal your intentions clearly
  • Choose the correct lane on approach

Turning Left (1st Exit)

  • Approach in the left lane
  • Signal left on approach
  • Keep the signal on throughout
  • Stay in the left lane on the roundabout
  • Exit when safe

Going Straight (2nd Exit)

  • Approach in the left lane (unless road markings indicate otherwise)
  • No signal on approach
  • Signal left after passing the exit before yours
  • Stay in the left lane
  • Exit when safe

Turning Right (3rd Exit or More)

  • Approach in the right lane
  • Signal right on approach
  • Stay in the right lane on the roundabout
  • Signal left after passing the exit before yours
  • Check your left mirror and blind spot before exiting
  • Move to the left lane to exit
💡 Remember MSPSL: Mirror, Signal, Position, Speed, Look

Multi-Lane Roundabouts

  • Always follow lane markings and road signs
  • Choose your lane based on your exit
  • Don't change lanes on the roundabout unless necessary
  • Be extra careful of vehicles in adjacent lanes

Mini Roundabouts

  • Same rules apply as normal roundabouts
  • Slow down and be prepared to stop
  • Give way to traffic from the right
  • Be aware that some drivers may go straight over without slowing
⚠️ Watch Out For: Cyclists and motorcyclists may stay in the left lane even when turning right – always check your mirrors and blind spots before changing lanes or exiting.

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • ❌ Entering when it's not safe (always give way to the right)
  • ❌ Wrong lane selection on approach
  • ❌ Not signalling when exiting
  • ❌ Cutting across lanes on the roundabout
  • ❌ Going too fast to react safely
💡 Practice Tip: Practice on quiet roundabouts during off-peak hours until you build confidence. Your instructor can help you understand the specific layout of roundabouts in your area.

💷 Learner Driver Insurance Explained

Understanding insurance is crucial for learner drivers. Here's everything you need to know about getting insured and keeping costs down.

Types of Learner Insurance

1. Named Driver on Parent's Policy

  • Most affordable option for regular practice
  • Added to parent/guardian's existing insurance
  • Can only drive the specified vehicle
  • Must be supervised by qualified driver (over 21, held licence 3+ years)
  • Affects parent's no-claims bonus if you have an accident

2. Short-Term Learner Insurance

  • Hourly, daily, or weekly cover
  • Ideal for occasional practice sessions
  • Doesn't affect parent's policy
  • More expensive per hour but flexible
  • Can insure multiple vehicles

3. Own Policy Insurance

  • Your own policy on your own car
  • Most expensive option for learners
  • Builds your own no-claims discount
  • Useful if you plan to drive immediately after passing

Insurance Requirements While Learning

  • Must have at least third-party insurance to drive on public roads
  • Your driving instructor's car is already insured for lessons
  • You need separate insurance for practice with family/friends
  • L-plates must be displayed when driving
⚠️ Legal Requirement: Driving without valid insurance is illegal and can result in 6-8 penalty points, a fine, and a driving ban.

How to Reduce Insurance Costs

1. Black Box (Telematics) Insurance

  • Device fitted to monitor driving behaviour
  • Rewards safe driving with lower premiums
  • Can reduce costs by 20-30%
  • Monitors speed, braking, cornering, time of day
  • Some policies set curfews (e.g., no driving 11pm-5am)

2. Pass Plus Course

  • 6-module advanced driving course after passing test
  • Covers motorways, night driving, adverse conditions
  • Many insurers offer discounts (10-35%)
  • Costs £150-200 but can save more than this

3. Higher Voluntary Excess

  • Agree to pay more if you claim
  • Reduces monthly/annual premium
  • Don't set it higher than you can afford
  • Typical learner excess: £200-500

4. Build Driving Experience

  • Some insurers offer discounts if you've had lessons for 6+ months
  • Passing first time can reduce premiums
  • Advanced driving courses add credibility

5. Choose Your Car Wisely

  • Smaller engines (1.0-1.2L) are cheaper to insure
  • Lower insurance groups (1-10) cost less
  • Avoid modified or high-performance cars
  • Older cars aren't always cheaper (safety features matter)
💡 Money-Saving Tip: Pay annually instead of monthly if possible – monthly payments often include interest charges.

What Affects Your Premium

  • Age: Under 25s pay significantly more
  • Location: Urban areas have higher rates than rural
  • Vehicle type: Engine size, value, security features
  • Annual mileage: Lower mileage = lower premium
  • Where car is kept: Garage is cheaper than street parking
  • Occupation: Some jobs are seen as lower risk

After Your Test

  • Insurance typically drops by 20-40% after passing
  • Shop around – don't auto-renew
  • Build your no-claims bonus (1 year = 30% discount, 5 years = 60%+)
  • Consider dashcam insurance discounts
  • Update your policy immediately after passing
⚠️ Important: If you have an accident while learning, report it to your insurance company within 24 hours – even if you weren't at fault and don't plan to claim.

Common Insurance Mistakes

  • ❌ Not declaring modifications (even cosmetic)
  • ❌ Incorrect mileage estimates
  • ❌ Failing to update address
  • ❌ Not comparing quotes from multiple providers
  • ❌ Assuming the cheapest is always best (check what's covered)
