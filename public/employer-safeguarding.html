Know Your Responsibilities

1. Employees Who Drive as Part of Their Job

Deliveries, site visits, sales calls, inter-site travel, company or personal vehicles used for business

Your safeguarding responsibilities:

  • Verify they're legal to drive for you. Valid licence, appropriate insurance (business use), no disqualifications. Not "assumed"—checked and documented.
  • Assess fitness to drive. Medical conditions, fatigue, eyesight, medication. If they crash because you didn't ask, you own that.
  • Confirm the vehicle is safe. MOT, servicing, tyres, lights. Doesn't matter if it's their car or yours—if it's a work journey, it's your problem.
  • Train them on your expectations. Journey planning, mobile phone policy, what to do in a collision. A quick chat doesn't count. Documented training does.
  • Have a post-incident process that actually works. Not a vague policy PDF. A system that captures facts, classifies the journey, and protects both the driver and the business when seconds matter.
  • Monitor and review. Regular checks, incident trends, driver feedback. If you're not tracking it, you're not managing it.
  • Respond when something goes wrong. Clear support, investigation, lessons learned. Silence or delay tells your team you don't care—and tells a court you weren't serious about duty of care.

Bottom line: If they crash on your time, the first question you'll be asked is "What did you do to prevent this?" You better have an answer.

This app is that answer. It switches on the second a crash happens, captures everything that matters, and turns chaos into a defensible incident file. If you're serious about protecting your drivers and your business, this is the system that proves it.

2. Employees Who Drive to Get to Work

Commuting, home-to-office, standard journeys that aren't part of the job itself

Your safeguarding responsibilities:

  • Generally, you're not liable for standard commuting. Employee leaves home, drives to your office, clocks in—that's on them, not you.
  • But the grey zones will destroy you if you're not careful:
    • Employee drives straight from home to a client site? Work journey.
    • Employee gets called back in after hours for an urgent job? Work journey.
    • Employee told to "just drop this off on your way home"? Work journey.
    • Employee uses their own car but you reimburse mileage? Potential work journey.
  • Your responsibility: Know the difference and capture it in real time. If there's a collision and you can't prove whether it was commuting or work-related, you're in a legal and insurance nightmare. Months later, memories are useless and positions have hardened.
  • Have a system that clarifies journey type immediately. Not retroactive guesswork. Instant classification at the time of the incident, with structured evidence to back it up.
  • Communicate your policy clearly. Employees need to know what counts as a work journey and what doesn't. "We'll figure it out later" is not a policy—it's an exposure.
  • Support employees even when it's not your liability. If someone crashes on their commute, how you respond still defines your culture. Are you empathetic and helpful, or do you shrug and say "not our problem"?

Bottom line: The law might say commuting isn't your responsibility—until the journey becomes a grey zone and suddenly it is. If you can't prove what type of journey it was, you lose. Simple as that.

This app kills the grey zone. It asks the right questions, classifies the journey instantly, and gives you bulletproof documentation before anyone's memory fades or positions harden. No guessing. No disputes. Just facts that hold up when it matters.
